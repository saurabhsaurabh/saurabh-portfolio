01 — ABOUT

From engineering to strategy.

Coming from a family with a long tradition of government service, I chose engineering instead — studying Biotechnology at Delhi Technological University, and becoming the first engineer in my family. Countless assignments, practicals, vivas and exams drilled a quantitative, analytical instinct into me that's stuck ever since.

That same appetite for unfamiliar territory carried into my first job — interning as an engineer at a law firm, of all places. I came in short on patience, expecting clients to move at my pace. My supervisor corrected that, and I learned to meet people where they are instead.

Then came the defining chapter: joining Epicere's four-person founding team as its fifth member, in a field none of us had properly trained for. My title said Marketing Strategist, but the work spanned marketing, strategy, sales, and operations at once. Store visits taught me more than dashboards ever did — the distance between assumption and reality is wider than it looks.

In 2024, I stepped away to care for a close family member, and spent 13 months volunteering with Jijeevisha, working with women and children in a Delhi rag-pickers' settlement — both taught me more about patience and empathy than any job has.

In August 2025, I joined ESSEC. Paris has since become home — apprenticeship applications, an ongoing immersion into French life, and coursework spanning Elle & Vire, Revolut's scorecard, and Smart Wanderers. But it's the fan-loyalty strategy I built for the Belgian national football team that's stayed with me most — a reminder that football, or the wider entertainment and media industry, is where I'm genuinely hoping to build a career, not just a case study I once wrote.

07 — BEYOND THE WORK

What I do when I'm not doing this.

Football & Cricket

Cricket found me first — I was three, and it's my older brother's fault. It stayed with me competitively through school, until the summer of 2014 handed my heart to someone new: Real Madrid won La Décima that year, and I haven't looked back since. Football is where most of my attention goes now — I'm not going to pretend I'm any good at playing it, but I understand the game better than most who are, and that love has grown well past the 90 minutes into how the sport works commercially and how it moves people. Working in football, in some capacity, is one of two dream roles I'm chasing. Cricket, meanwhile, is different — I don't watch it much anymore, but I can still follow the game at the same level as someone who does, because it's not really something I learned. I grew up inside it.

Tennis

I watched Federer's whole second prime — the comeback after 2016 was one of the great sports stories of my lifetime — and I still haven't fully recovered from the 2019 Wimbledon final, two championship points from history, lost to Djokovic in a match that still haunts me a little.

Cinema

Cinema is my second language, and that started early. I was partly raised by a television — sitting in front of it as a kid with zero control over what was actually playing, and somehow still falling completely in love with the old classic Hindi films that happened to be on. Once I actually got a say in what I watched, that catalogue just kept expanding — other regional Indian cinema, then British and American films. These days I'll watch something in a language I don't speak a word of, subtitles the only bridge, because good world-building doesn't need a shared vocabulary to pull you somewhere else entirely.

Current affairs, politics & history

I follow current affairs, politics and history closely, partly because the world feels more volatile than it used to, and partly because I think staying informed is less a hobby than a responsibility — to your own judgment, and to the people around you. I believe pacifism deserves more advocates than it gets. And history, cliché as it sounds, is the one record we have of what we got right and what we got catastrophically wrong — ignoring it doesn't feel like an option.

For more than two years, I also served as a caregiver for a close family member — work that never shows up on a CV, but taught me more about empathy and patience than anything else here.
